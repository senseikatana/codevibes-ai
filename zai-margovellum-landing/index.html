KILTER
RECORDS

“A label that never
sits still.”

Kilter presses records by artists who can't be pinned down, so the identity refuses to be either. The mark is three misregistered strokes — a K mid-collapse — redrawn at every size, never twice the same.

The system ships as rules, not artwork: weights, angles and a construction grid any intern can break correctly.

FERMENT

“Shelf presence
without shouting.”

Thirty-one SKUs, one grid. Ferment's jars are labelled like specimen cards — latin binomials at 8 pt, flavour at 190 pt — because the product is alive and the design should look like it knows.

No photography. The typography does the tasting.

№ 04EDITORIAL / BOOK DESIGN2025

ASFALT
PRESS

“Books built
like overpasses.”

A publisher of writing about cities. The grid takes its logic from road markings — lane widths, dashes, merge arrows — and holds 320 pages together without a single ornament.

The colophon is set in the typeface of highway signage. Obviously.

CLIENT — ASFALT PRESS
SCOPE — SERIES GRID, 6 TITLES
INK — PURPLE 265
ASFALT PRESS — FALL 2025ESSAYS III
Cities are read at the speed of traffic. The underpass is a paragraph; the overpass, a footnote that got ambitious. This issue collects eleven essays on concrete, wayfinding, and the particular loneliness of parking garages at three in the morning. None of the writers drive. All of them walk the same ring road, at different hours, taking notes in the margins of free transit maps they refuse to pay for. The result is a guidebook to a city that keeps repaving itself mid-sentence.
