There's a particular kind of disappointment that comes from a phone that's almost right. The Aspect Mini Pro is the most frustrating device I've tested this year — not because it's bad, but because it's _so close_ to being the answer to a question we've been asking for half a decade: can a small phone still be a flagship?

### Build Quality & In-Hand Feel

At 168g with a titanium frame, it's the first compact since the iPhone 13 Mini that doesn't feel like a concession. My thumb reaches the top corner. The phone disappears into a jeans pocket.

### The Display: A Reality Check

On paper: 6.1" LTPO, 1–120Hz, 2400 nits peak. Measured with a Sekonic C-800, sustained outdoor brightness runs ~15% under the iPhone 15 Pro. Peak numbers mislead; sustained brightness is what you feel in sunlight.

### The Battery Question

21 days averaged **6 hours 42 minutes** screen-on time with 1.8%/h standby drain. Gaming for 30 minutes costs 22% — physics wins in a chassis this small. For non-gamers: a non-issue.

### The Verdict

Not perfect — dimmer outdoors than the best, weaker low-light than Pixel/iPhone, $899 against bigger rivals. And yet I keep reaching for it, because it disappears. If you've waited for a small phone that doesn't suck, this is it.
